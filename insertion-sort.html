🃏 Insertion Sort

Take items one at a time and slide each into its correct place among the ones already sorted.

Everyday picture: sorting playing cards in your hand. You pick up a new card and slide it left past the bigger cards until it fits. The cards in your hand are always in order; the cards still on the table aren't yet.

Watch it

Switch the data to Nearly sorted: insertion sort flies through it, because each new item has barely anywhere to go. This is its superpower.

How it works

  1. Treat the first item as a sorted "hand" of one card.
  2. Take the next item and compare it with the one to its left.
  3. If the left one is bigger, swap and keep sliding left. Stop when the left neighbour is smaller (or you reach the start).
  4. The sorted hand has grown by one. Repeat for every remaining item.

Real life

🃏 Cards in your hand
The classic example — most people do this instinctively.
🏭 Inside real sorting functions
Fast hybrid sorts (like Timsort, used by Python and by Java for objects) use insertion sort for short chunks, because it's very quick on small or nearly-sorted data.
📥 Live data
When new items arrive one by one into an already-sorted list.

Use it when the list is small, nearly sorted, or items arrive one at a time.
Avoid it when the list is large and in random order — reversed data is its worst case.
