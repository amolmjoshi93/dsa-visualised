🎯 Selection Sort

Find the smallest item in the unsorted part, put it at the front. Then find the next smallest. Repeat.

Everyday picture: picking a cricket team by height. Scan everyone, choose the shortest, send them to the front of the line. Scan the rest, choose the shortest again, put them second. Each round, one person is permanently in their final place.

Watch it

Look at the "Swaps / writes" counter: selection sort does very few swaps (at most one per round), even though it compares a lot.

How it works

  1. Scan the unsorted part and remember where the smallest value is (the orange bar is the current "smallest so far").
  2. Swap it with the first unsorted position.
  3. That position is now final (green). Shrink the unsorted part by one.
  4. Repeat until everything is placed.

Real life

🏆 Ranking a leaderboard by hand
Pick the best, then the best of the rest.
💾 Writes are expensive
Because it makes the fewest swaps, it suits situations where moving data costs far more than looking at it (some flash-memory cases).

Speed at a glance

CaseSpeed

Stable?No (swapping can jump equal items over each other)
Use it when the list is small and swaps are costly.
Avoid it when the list is large, or nearly sorted — it can't exploit that, unlike insertion sort.
