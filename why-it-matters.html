🚀 Why does any of this matter?

With 10 items, every method feels instant. The differences only explode when the data gets big — and real data always does.

Everyday picture: finding a name in a phone book. With 20 names, flipping through is fine. With 20 million names, flipping through takes a lifetime — but opening in the middle and halving takes about 25 looks. Same task, wildly different effort, purely because of how you go about it.

The big idea

  • Choosing a good data structure decides which operations are cheap: arrays love "read by position", hash tables love "find by key", trees love "keep sorted and search".
  • Choosing a good algorithm decides how the work grows as data grows.
  • Big-O notation is just a way to describe that growth, ignoring small details. O(n²) vs O(n log n) is the difference between done in a blink and go make tea — or years.
  • Nobody memorises every detail. The skill is knowing the trade-offs so you pick sensibly.
