🫧 Bubble Sort

Walk along the line, swap any two neighbours that are in the wrong order, and repeat. The biggest values "bubble up" to the end.

Everyday picture: students lined up for a photo, supposed to be shortest to tallest. You walk down the line and whenever someone is taller than the person next to them, you make them swap. After one walk the tallest student is at the end. After another walk, the second tallest is in place. Keep going until a full walk needs no swaps.

Watch it

Try the Nearly sorted data option — bubble sort notices when a pass needs no swaps and stops early. Then try Reversed — its worst nightmare.

How it works

  1. Compare the first two items. If the left one is bigger, swap them.
  2. Move one step right and compare the next pair. Continue to the end of the list.
  3. The largest item is now at the end (green). It never needs to move again.
  4. Repeat for the remaining items. If a whole pass makes no swaps, the list is sorted — stop.

🎓 Teaching
Bubble sort is rarely used in real software, but it's a perfect first example of how a sorting algorithm thinks.

Speed at a glance

CaseSpeed
Best (already sorted)O(n) — one pass, no swaps
AverageO(n²)
Worst (reversed)O(n²)
Extra memoryO(1) — sorts in place
Stable?Yes — equal items keep their order

O(n²) means: 10× more items → about 100× more work. See that on the Why it matters page.

Use it when learning, or for tiny lists that are almost sorted.
Avoid it when the list is large. Use merge sort or quick sort.
